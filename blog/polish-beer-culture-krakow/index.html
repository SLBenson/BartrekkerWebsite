Polish Beer Culture In Kraków

12 September 2026 • 10 min read • By The Bartrekker Team

A Brief History Of Beer In Poland

Beer has been part of Polish culture for centuries. During the Middle Ages, it was commonly produced locally and was an important drink in towns and villages across the country due to its high calorific value and the limited access to clean, safe drinking water.

Kraków was an important brewing centre, with breweries, taverns and the city's brewers' guild forming part of medieval life. Beer was not simply something enjoyed on a night out; it was part of everyday food and social culture. Beer brewing was taken very seriously in Kraków with the city guild monitoring quality with the threat of shaming or banishment.

Over time, brewing became increasingly industrialised. Large Polish breweries grew into the major brands familiar today, including Tyskie, Żywiec, Warka, Lech and Okocim.

However, Polish beer culture has changed significantly again in recent decades.

Beer Culture Across Polish Cities

Polish beer culture isn't exactly the same everywhere.

Kraków is particularly well known for its craft beer bars, helped by its large student population, tourism industry and busy nightlife. The Old Town and Kazimierz have a high concentration of bars where you can find both Polish classics and beers from independent breweries.

Regional influences matter. Different parts of Poland have their own breweries, traditions and local favourites, meaning that exploring beer can be another way to discover new flavours whilst experiencing different areas of Poland.

In Warsaw there is Browary Warszawskie with their flagship Browar Warszawski brewpub, they create modern recreations of historic local styles, notably their traditional Baltic Porters, Grodziskie (a historic Polish smoked wheat ale), and Bavarian-style wheat beers. Tapping into historical beer culture from Poland.

Polish Craft Beer: What Should You Try?

Polish craft breweries produce an enormous variety of beers, but there are a few styles worth looking out for.

Grodziskie

Grodziskie is one of Poland's most distinctive traditional beer styles. Originally associated with Grodzisk Wielkopolski, it is a light, highly carbonated wheat beer made using smoked malt. This beer is refreshing and noticeably smoky.

Baltic Porter

Baltic Porter can be dark, strong and rich, with hints of chocolate, coffee, roasted malt and dark fruit. Poland has a particularly strong tradition of brewing this style.

Polish Craft IPA

If you prefer modern craft beer, look for a Polish IPA. These can range from bitter, traditional IPAs to modern versions packed with citrus and tropical hop flavours. This is the most common version of craft beer being brewed today, so why not try a polish IPA?

If you visit a brewery, a brewpub or just a craft beer pub you will find that the beers on tap change regularly so you will always get to try something fresh.

Where to Try Polish Beer in Kraków

Kraków has plenty of places to explore Polish beer, particularly around the Old Town and Kazimierz.

C.K. Browar is a brewpub close to the Old Town where beer is brewed on site, making it a good option if you want to experience the traditional brewpub concept.

In Kazimierz, Nowy Kraftowy and Weźże Krafta are popular choices for discovering modern craft beers from Poland and beyond.

For a large selection in the Old Town, Multi Qlti Tap Bar offers numerous beers on tap, making it easy to try different styles in one location.

Whether you're looking for a traditional Polish lager, a locally brewed beer or something completely experimental, Kraków gives you plenty to choose from.

Poland's beer culture has come a long way from its medieval breweries and traditional lagers. Today, historic brewing traditions exist alongside a growing craft beer scene. Kraków is one of the best places to experience both.
