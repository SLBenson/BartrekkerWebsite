25 Best Things To Do On Your Visit To Kraków

11 September 2026 • 10 Minute Read • By The Bartrekker Team

Kraków is one of the most beautiful cities in Poland, maybe even Europe, with a rich diversity of activities and beautiful locations, there is no wonder tourism is increasing each year. This list gives you an idea of some of the most interesting things to do and see in and around Kraków.

1. The Main Square

The main square in Kraków is known as the largest medieval square in Europe covering around 40,000 sq/m with large open spaces and beautiful architecture surrounding the Cloth hall (Sukiennice), a central renaissance market, still operating today, selling a variety of traditional jewellery and clothing. On the northeast corner you will find the Bazylika Mariacka catholic church, if you are lucky you will catch the cultural trumpet song that is played from the tallest tower of the church.

2. Wawel The Royal Castle of Kraków

Placed overlooking the Vistula River is the Wawel Royal Castle, built and designed with a blend of Medieval, renaissance and Baroque styles, it was the home of Polish kings for centuries. The castle was one of the first buildings along with Kraków’s centre to be named a Unesco World Heritige Site in 1978.

3. The Fire Breathing Dragon of Wawel (Smok Wawelski)

The legend says that a dragon lived in a cave under the Wawel Hill and scared the people of Kraków, the legend was brought to life in 1972 when an artist created a six-meter bronze dragon sculpture that breathes fire. To see the dragon you walk along the river at the base of the castle. Find it here.

4. Poland's Oldest Botanical Garden

Sitting very close to the centre of Kraków is Poland’s oldest Botanical garden, The Botanic Garden of the Jagiellonian university boasts a huge number of over 8,000 species and a variety of extremely rare plants. When walking around the gardens it gives a magical secret garden feel. If you fancy yourself a botanist you won’t want to skip this.

5. Kraków's Many Mounds

If you want some of the most amazing views of Kraków you have some great options, surrounding the city are artificial hills that were built originally as tombs for ancient rulers, more recently they were leveraged as military vantage points and forts. However in the modern day they give the best views of the city and the surrounding areas with them being situated higher than the architecture. The reason the mounds have such a clear view of the city centre is due to strict sightline protection zones that preserve the sightline to the centre of the city, so city planners and architects need to adhere to these rules when planning construction. Visit the closest here.

6. Kazimierz, The Cultural Jewish Quarter

Kazimierz is Krakóws trendiest and most historically rich area, located just south of the Old town (Main Square). The square (Plac Nowy) in Kazimierz is a vibrant social hub if you want to escape the hustle of the Main Square, known for the diverse selection of nightlife, daytime flea markets and the evening food stalls that sell Zapiekanki (open toasted sandwiches) which are absolutely delicious for an evening snack. Kazimierz is host to some of the best restaurants in Kraków so make sure to do your research as there are lots of them!.

7. Tasting Traditional Polish Food

What is the point in coming to Kraków for a trip if you don’t taste what Polish cuisine has to offer. Eastern European food is an interesting mix of flavours that you won’t find in many other places in the world. With Western Europe having a variety of cultures influencing their cuisine in some way, the cuisine in Poland has heavily stuck to its roots with a lot of the restaurants keeping the traditional style, decor and original recipes. Foods you have to try whilst you visit Kraków are:

  • Pierogi - Polish dumplings, can be made savoury or sweet, with the most popular version called Ruskie filled with potato and cheese or the sweet option filled with strawberries and topped with a sweet cream.
  • Żurek - Sour Rye Soup, a sour soup served with sausages and a hard-boiled egg, it is one of the most popular soups in Poland.
  • Kotlet Schabowy - Breaded, fried pork cutlet. Very similar to a schnitzel, usually served with potato and pickled cabbage.

8. Partying On A Pub Crawl

Kraków is the perfect place if you enjoy nightlife, with lots of pubs, bars and clubs all concentrated a short distance from each other, however this is a blessing and a curse, how do you choose a place to go?. Kraków is home to multiple pub crawls offering slightly different experiences, with some offering unlimited beer, gin, whisky, rum and vodka for one hour before guiding you to multiple bars, clubs and even the occasional karaoke visit. Here are 3 of the top pub crawls in Kraków:

  • Krawl Through Krakow
  • Krakow Crawl
  • Krakow Animals Crawl

9. The Shooting Range

Have you ever wanted to try out shooting a real gun? Kraków has an indoor shooting range located extremely close to the centre. The shooting range gives you access to a large variety of guns to choose from after you take part in a mandatory safety briefing and instructional session. This is truly a unique experience and very rare to find so close to a city centre.

10. Indoor Axe Throwing

Do you want to let out your inner viking with another unique experience located directly in the centre of Krakow. The number one axe throwing location in Poland is the perfect place. Improve your ability and technique with the advice of professional instructors that will help you hone in your skills.

12. Boat On The Vistula River

The Vistula River runs through the heart of Kraków, the river is Poland’s longest waterway and it runs very close to some amazing monuments. If you would like to experience the sights from the water there are plenty of options for you to experience, dine in one of the many floating restaurants along the rivers edge, float along the river on a river cruise or if you want something a little different in the warmer seasons, hop on the boat party for 2-hours of booze filled fun.

13. Zakrzowek, Swim In The Old Quarry

If you like to swim, paddleboard or cold dip, this beautiful location is for you, situated around 3km from the centre of the city is an old quarry that naturally filled with clean water over time and is now used as a popular recreational spot for locals and tourists. There are floating pools with decking that are monitored by lifeguards through the summer months. It’s a popular spot for cold dipping in the winter too. Located here.

14. Forum By The River

If you want to find somewhere to settle down and refuel, then Forum is the perfect place, set just next to the river with a perfect landscape view of Wawel castle is Forum Food & Fun, featuring multiple bars that serve high quality cocktails, a food hall style restaurant setup with cuisines from mexican, pizza, burgers to polish and sushi, a cafe serving cakes, endless pool tables and even a bowling alley. If you are there on a warm day you can take a load off and sit on the deck chairs out front of the building facing directly at Wawel castle for an unforgettable view. If you are not afraid of heights you can take a trip to the sky in the famous hot air balloon and get some of the best aerial views you can find anywhere in the city.

15. Gelato On Florienska Street

If you are a lover of Ice-cream then Rinella is the small hidden spot you never knew you needed, a family owned factory and shop with the best gelato you can taste outside of Italy. With up to 24 different flavours of Gelato and Sorbet you can choose the size of ice-cream you want. There is also vegan (lactose free) gelato in two flavours to choose from which tastes the same if not better at times than original gelato (we don’t know how they do it) They are open all year round, yes, even in the winter making this a great spot to visit any time you are on Florienska street. Find it here.

16. The Wieliczka & Bochnia Salt Mines

An important piece of history for Kraków are the salt mines, two locations dug deep underground with the main spot being situated in Wieliczka and the secondary less popular spot in Bochnia. Wieliczka salt mine was decommissioned in 1996 but has been used as a tourist attraction since and for good reason, the amazing spectacle features over 2,000 chambers, underground lakes, and intricate salt-carved chapels with the most famous being St. Kinga's Chapel: A massive underground cathedral carved entirely from salt, complete with chandeliers and altars (try not to lick the walls).

17. Auschwitz Concentration Camp

One major part of world war II history in Poland is the number of concentration camps still standing, with the most famous and historical being Auschwitz, split into two locations very close to each other. You can take a short trip from Kraków with an organised trip or take a train to discover the dark history of one of the biggest atrocities known to the modern world. We would suggest taking a whole day to visit as there is a vast amount to learn about the events that occurred there.

18. The Unity Eye

The Unity Tower is one of the most recently renovated buildings in Kraków located in a corporate section of the city just set outside the centre, this building defies any resemblance to the rest of the architecture in Kraków as it towers over the buildings nearby, with 26 floors it is the tallest building in the city. You would be fooled if you thought it was only offices, the top two floors have been reserved for a Restaurant & Bar called The Unity Eye with the perfect panoramic view of Kraków, you won’t get a better view from any other building. The restaurant and bar offer the finest and highest quality food and drinks a restaurant has to offer and with a view like this one you will redefine the meaning of “High end” dining.

19. The Rynek Underground Museum

Walking through the Main Square in Kraków you will most likely miss this incredible piece of history right beneath your feet, this museum connects excavated medieval streets covered with walkway ramps made of glass covering 11th-to-13th-century cobblestone roads, foundational walls, and original merchant stalls, giving you an insight into real Polish history. Tickets are limited with this one so you should book in advance. If you are lucky enough to be in Kraków in mid May, there is a chance you may catch the Free museum day where all museums in Kraków are free to enter with special opening hours and programmes.
