Visiting Kraków for the first time? The city is filled with beautiful locations, from a medieval market square to a riverside castle and a former Jewish quarter that's now full of bars and street food. Here are the ten spots our guides send every first-time visitor to see.

1. Rynek Główny (Main Market Square)

One of the largest medieval squares in Europe, and still the beating heart of the city. Grab a bench, order a coffee from one of the surrounding cafés, and watch Kraków go by — street musicians, horse-drawn carriages and all.

2. St. Mary's Basilica

The twin-towered brick church overlooking the square is famous for its wooden altarpiece and the hourly trumpet call — the hejnał — played live from the taller tower, which cuts off mid-note in honour of a 13th-century trumpeter shot mid-warning.

3. Wawel Royal Castle

Perched on a hill above the Vistula River, Wawel was the residence of Polish kings for centuries. Walk the castle grounds for free, or pay for entry to the State Rooms, Royal Private Apartments, or the dragon's den beneath the hill.

4. Kazimierz (the Jewish Quarter)

South of the Old Town, Kazimierz has gone from historic Jewish district to one of the most atmospheric nightlife areas in the city. By day it's synagogues and museums; by night, some of the best bars on a Bartrekker route.

5. Rynek Underground Museum

Beneath the Main Square lies an entire archaeological museum built around the medieval trading routes unearthed during renovation work — a genuinely surprising way to spend an hour out of the sun (or rain).

6. Schindler's Factory

Oskar Schindler's former enamel factory is now a museum covering Kraków's occupation during the Second World War. It's a heavier stop than the rest of this list, but one of the most highly rated things to do in the city.

"You could spend a week in Kraków and still not run out of side streets worth getting lost in."

7. Cloth Hall (Sukiennice)

The Renaissance trading hall running down the middle of the Main Square has sold cloth, spices and souvenirs for over 700 years. Today it's mostly amber jewellery and Polish crafts — still worth a browse.

8. Kościuszko Mound

A short tram ride from the centre, this man-made hill was raised in the 1820s in honour of national hero Tadeusz Kościuszko. The climb is easy and the panoramic view over Kraków at the top is one of the best in the city.

9. Barbican & St. Florian's Gate

One of the few surviving pieces of Kraków's medieval fortifications, the circular Barbican once guarded the northern entrance to the city. Walk through St. Florian's Gate afterwards onto Floriańska Street, the Old Town's main artery.

10. Planty Park

A green ring circling the entire Old Town where the city walls used to stand. It's the easiest way to walk between sights without retracing your steps — and a good place to slow down between stops on a busy day.
